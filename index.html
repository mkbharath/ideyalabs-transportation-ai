Executive Summary

Fund three now, five in the initial portfolio, seven over the medium term

Eight of the fifteen portfolio solutions turn out to be an earlier, less autonomous version of one of the ten agents. This document consolidates both source analyses into a single 18-product portfolio, scores every product on a common framework, and recommends a deliberately narrow, sequenced build so that reuse compounds.

The conclusion is decisive. Fund three products immediately — Invoice Audit & Payment Agent, Shipment Exception Management Agent, and Freight Claims Management & Recovery Agent — because together they are the fastest to prove, the most self-funding, and (in Exception Management’s case) the largest, most durable platform bet on the list. A further five follow in a tightly sequenced Build-Next wave that reuses infrastructure the first three already require.

The biggest risk is sequencing risk. Building Exception Management, Fraud Prevention, or any Tier 2/3 agent before the Invoice Audit Agent has proven the dispute-and-recovery pattern would forfeit the compounding-reuse advantage this roadmap is built around.
Decision at a Glance

Twelve questions leadership asks first, answered directly

If we can fund only 3, which three?
Invoice Audit & Payment Agent, Shipment Exception Management Agent, Freight Claims Management & Recovery Agent.
If we can fund 5, which five?
The above three, plus Carrier Identity & Fraud-Prevention Agent and FreightLink (Visibility Hub).
Which one is the flagship?
Invoice Audit, Discrepancy Resolution & Payment Agent.
Best short-term revenue opportunity?
Invoice Audit & Payment Agent (self-funding); Claims Agent is the strongest second option.
Best long-term platform opportunity?
Shipment Exception Management Agent (agent layer) built on FreightLink + Bridge (infrastructure layer).
Fastest path to a customer pilot?
FreightLink — the fastest, lowest-effort MVP of any solution across both source documents.
Strongest measurable ROI?
Invoice Audit & Payment Agent and Freight Claims Management Agent — both self-funding, both provable within a single pilot cycle.
Strongest competitive whitespace?
Freight Claims Management & Recovery Agent — rated White Space, the least crowded opportunity identified in either source document.
What should be combined?
AuditGuard + DisputeResolve → Invoice Audit Agent; DockFlow → Detention & Dock Agent; ClaimsPilot → Claims Agent; RateIQ → Quoting Agent; ETAsense → Exception Management Agent; OnboardIQ → Carrier Fraud Agent (first release).
What should not be standalone products?
Last-Mile Delivery Agent (Red Ocean) and the six merged-away solutions; CubeOptimizer, ShipperConnect, ComplyCore, and GreenMetrics are deferred, not eliminated.
Which shared platform capabilities come first?
Document intelligence (invoice/RFQ/claim parsing) and the dispute-and-negotiation engine, both required by Invoice Audit and immediately reusable by Claims; FreightLink’s ingestion and Bridge’s TMS/EDI gateway follow next.
What should we explicitly not build first?
Last-Mile Delivery, Cross-Border Customs, GreenMetrics, ComplyCore, ShipperConnect, CubeOptimizer, CapacityMatch, and DispatchCopilot — all real opportunities, none is where the early engineering effort should go.

Overlap & Consolidation

25 original solutions → 18 unique products

In every pair below the Source B agent describes a more autonomous version of the same problem a Source A solution addresses. Six are a full merge; the rest are kept separate or absorbed as a module.

Same billing-error problem; AuditGuard flags for review, the Agent drafts/negotiates/pays. AuditGuard’s gain-share pricing becomes the consolidated model.

DisputeResolve → Invoice Audit & Payment Agent Module

Source A calls it a “narrower companion to AuditGuard”; absorbed as a documented sub-capability, not a separate product.

ETAsense → Shipment Exception Mgmt Agent Merge

The Agent’s own architecture names predictive-ETA as its perception layer — that is ETAsense.

FreightLink · Shipment Exception Mgmt Agent Separate (layered)

FreightLink is the data/visibility foundation; the Exception Agent is the reasoning-and-action layer on top. Both kept as distinct products.

OnboardIQ → Carrier Fraud & Onboarding Agent Merge (first release)

Source B’s own MVP scope is onboarding-verification first — that first release is OnboardIQ.

RateIQ → Freight Quoting & RFQ Agent Merge

RateIQ’s tariff-ingestion and NMFC-classification logic becomes the agent’s deterministic rating core.

DockFlow → Detention & Dock Coordination Agent Merge

DockFlow’s appointment-portal UX becomes the data-capture layer feeding the agent’s reasoning/action layer.

ClaimsPilot → Freight Claims Mgmt Agent Merge

ClaimsPilot’s intake/triage UX becomes the consolidated agent’s human-facing layer; the Agent is the proactive version.

CapacityMatch · Empty-Mile / Backhaul Agent Separate (complementary)

Opposite sides of the same marketplace for different buyers — not a duplicate; could share a lane/carrier-ranking model.

DispatchCopilot · Exception / Dispatch agents Separate (different product)

Advisory-only (“never auto-execute”); a candidate future UI layer, not a true agent.

Implementation Sequence

A priority-ordered build sequence, not a fixed calendar

Sequenced to maximize reuse: the first product deliberately builds the document-intelligence and dispute infrastructure every early follower needs, so each subsequent step costs less engineering time than the one before it. Each step begins once its dependencies are proven, not on a preset date.

Sequence 1Establish the Flagship

Invoice Audit, Discrepancy Resolution & Payment Agent MVP — single carrier/mode; a human approves every dispute at first, with autonomy expanding as accuracy is demonstrated.

Shared capabilities
Document intelligence (OCR/LLM invoice parsing); the core dispute/negotiation engine; enough of Bridge’s EDI templates to ingest one invoice feed.
Dependencies
None blocking — the deliberate first move, chosen because it depends on nothing else in this portfolio.
Pilot
3–5 design-partner shippers/3PLs with $10M+ freight spend.
Business KPI
$ recovered as % of audited spend; days from invoice receipt to resolved payment.
Not yet
Exception Mgmt, Claims, Carrier Fraud, or any Tier 3/4 product — resist parallelizing before the flagship proves the model.
Sequence 2Fast-Follow on Proven Infrastructure

Freight Claims & Recovery Agent (fast follow, reusing Sequence 1 infrastructure directly); begin FreightLink’s connector build for the top NA carrier feeds.

Shared capabilities
Extend the dispute/negotiation engine from Invoice Audit to Claims; begin the carrier-data/identity registry needed for Sequence 3.
Dependencies
Claims reuses Sequence 1’s document-intelligence and carrier-portal communication — where the sequencing pays off for the first time.
Pilot
Claims — 6–8 high-claims-volume customers; FreightLink — 10–15 design partners.
Not yet
Cross-Border Customs and Last-Mile Delivery Agent — both out of scope through Sequence 2.
Sequence 3Scale the Platform

Shipment Exception Management Agent (now FreightLink data exists to feed it); Carrier Identity & Fraud-Prevention Agent (now carrier-data + document intelligence exist); begin selling Bridge standalone to legacy-TMS customers.

Shared capabilities
Orchestration layer matures at Exception Mgmt’s scale; communication/notification layer built out.
Dependencies
Exception Mgmt depends on FreightLink (Sequence 2); Carrier Fraud depends on the carrier-data registry begun in Sequence 2.
Business KPI
OTIF %, exception resolution time; fraud loss avoided per $ of freight managed.
Not yet
Last-Mile, GreenMetrics, ComplyCore — no clear demand signal yet.
Sequence 4Expand the Portfolio

Dynamic Procurement and Detention & Dock Agents (both benefit from mature orchestration, communication, and dispute infrastructure); evaluate Quoting and Empty-Mile/Backhaul on design-partner demand; revisit Cross-Border Customs once compliance credibility exists.

Shared capabilities
Full analytics/feedback-loop and model-retraining pipeline; a mature rules/policy engine reusable across future agents.
Business KPI
Freight cost per mile vs. market; detention hours per stop, % of legitimate detention invoices collected.
Not yet
Last-Mile remains out of scope for now; GreenMetrics, ComplyCore, ShipperConnect, CubeOptimizer, CapacityMatch, DispatchCopilot deferred pending clearer demand.

Reuse compounds: Invoice Audit builds document intelligence and the dispute engine → Claims reuses both immediately → FreightLink builds the visibility layer in parallel → Exception Management reuses FreightLink plus the maturing orchestration layer → Carrier Fraud reuses the carrier-data registry → Procurement and Detention & Dock reuse nearly everything built in the sequences before them. By Sequence 4, the marginal engineering cost of a new agent is a fraction of what Sequence 1 cost.
